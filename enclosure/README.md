# Enclosure

## Print

Download these files and import them into your slicer at **100% scale**:

| File                                     | Quantity | Print orientation |
|------------------------------------------|---------:|-------------------|
| [Base](dialback-zero-base.stl)           |        1 | Floor down        |
| [Lid](dialback-zero-lid.stl)             |        1 | Roof down         |
| [Power-button cap](power-button-cap.stl) |        1 | On its flange     |
| [12 mm Pi spacer](pi-spacer-12mm.stl)    |        4 | Vertical          |

Use **a 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.2 mm layers and four perimeters**.

Also needed: **two M3 x 25 screws** and
**two M3 hex nuts**.

## Assemble

1. Fit the Pi's male header on its component side and the board's J1 female
   socket on the underside. Use the sockets in the [parts list](../docs/self-solder-parts.md).
2. Place the Pi on the base guide pins. Slide a printed spacer onto each pin.
3. Align J1 with the Pi header and press the board down evenly onto its supports.
4. Insert the button cap from inside the lid with its flange wings vertical.
   Rotate it 90° so the power symbol is upright, and check it moves freely.
5. Slide the two nuts into the lid pockets. Lower the lid over the connectors,
   keeping the cap aligned with the power switch.
6. Insert both screws from below and tighten lightly until the case closes.

Remove both screws to open the case. The screws do not pass through either board.

STL files: **CC0-1.0**.
